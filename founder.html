The Founder

Jay Carpenter · 40 · Sydney · Dad of 7 · Husband of 1

One day I got sick of wasting thoughts like they didn't matter because they are free.

This entire site is slowly being built publicly. I am inviting people as this evolves because I do not have an end product I am building. I am also not the system I built. Far from it. However, this is a place for me to express my worldview through systems, and researching them to the most fundamental levels I can comprehend.

Turns out, from many discussions this work has opened up for me, I am realising quickly I do not necessarily think like many others. Not in a disagreeable way (most of the time), but almost like my mind is hardwired in a way where inversion logic is a part of my general consideration process.

I am not a scientist, mathematician, or developer by training. I'm a systems thinker who started asking questions and wouldn't stop. Over the 40 years it took me to build this website, I have spent my time absorbing knowledge across domains of interest. Not hobbies, not passions, not formal studies. A mix of jobs working in and around systems with an unburning desire to understand how they truly work.

The Early Web

SECS Sovereign as a software substrate started as a few websites built in and around 97–2003. My first multipage websites built entirely from coding skills learnt on htmlgoodies.com. Open a blank notepad and build away. I would share the Angelfire and Geocities URLs with school friends and the kind people of #Cheers and other communities I frequented on mIRC.

Sorry if you were one of the many people who randomly had their CD-ROM drives open whilst you were giving some punk kid in Australia false ASL details. You are lucky that's all I did.

Those same skills became transferrable across coding business tools, building web apps and managing developer teams across a range of industries. Through ERP implementation support. WMS selection and integration. So many internal and external integrations. A love of systems and IT.

The Turning Point

When I realised the day job and a bunch of thoughts in my head weren't going to be enough to provide more of a life for my family, I started exploring.

In late 2025, after a stay in hospital, I recognised a deep need to change so much of my life. I came home and I swapped laptops. I pulled my favourite laptop out of a drawer, the one that is old enough to be starting high school, and I set up my patio table with a monitor and a laptop stand.

The meaning comes from the why, not the what yet.

The what was combining 18 months of AI tinkering and 40 years of my life's work and experience internalised, and externalising it through exploration with the tools available today.

The Substrate

First the substrate. A complete inversion of everything I didn't know about why every system I ever built had never achieved what I desired to build. I had the knowledge of what it should look like. I had the logic coded in all of my accumulated codebases. But I had never tried to synthesise all of those systems together.

When I did, the determinism I had been seeking from first principles was achieved.

The built system was a shell then of what it is now. But that is v1.0.0 to v1.9.0 of the substrate. There is more.

Seeing the Patterns

I had also spent months working with AI in a range of complex spreadsheets. I found AI to be brilliant at VBA optimisation and Excel helper column removal from complex spreadsheets. By finding the formula structures I was defining logically possible, I saw the brains of the LLM models. Once I started to notice patterns in the performance, that was my starting research point.

So I studied AI as a product. In parallel with putting the finishing touches on the substrate in commercial readiness, even without a commercial pathway. That would require a plan.

What Came Next

I wasn't ready for what came next. And it is another story in itself.

I turned the knowledge I gained from deconstructing and reverse engineering AI training methods and then started to point the AI tools towards research I cared about, to answer the deep questions that would help me understand the life I am existing as.

The fine structure constant fell out of the algebra, after a lot of hard work.

I mean, I had to map the osmotic process of the skin of the frog, through the governance principles only an apiarist would care about, through principles I had only read about and never had explained to me. Then turning the algebra, from the running substrate, and applying it to the cosmological processes I could see in my mind as I ingested the research, and turned the focus to understanding measurable certainties like α and π.

Then an initial 47-document biological research corpus on gestational oxygen timing and its role in developmental injury — from autism to SIDS to cancer predisposition. (See JJ's Fingerprint to understand how this is relevant. It is a write-up in itself.) The collapsed version of this research is the Gestational Timing Framework (GTF) page.

The Method

None of this was planned, none of it is completed. I followed the structure wherever it led, as far as I could comprehend, and I only stopped when I didn't understand. Concept by concept, I was building paper by paper, and forming a pattern of algebraic universality that becomes undeniable when considered against the corpus as a whole.

I used AI extensively as a research and development tool — not to generate ideas, but to test them, stress-test them, and translate them into formal language. The ideas are mine. The rigour is collaborative.

The system is the proof. The proof is the system. I am just a guy.

— Jay
