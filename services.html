Patterns I have run (anonymised)

Hyper-growth fulfilment

Scaled outbound operations from tens of people to well over a hundred. Performance systems that roughly doubled pick rates and lifted pack rates by ~75%. On-time despatch from the high-nineties into >99.9%. Category-first speed products (same-day interstate, Saturday delivery), live dashboards on ERP/WMS, multi-site fulfilment launch.

End-to-end supply chain

Purchasing, import, warehouse, freight, installation, customer experience as one system. Multi-year people / process / systems strategy with ownership models — change only when it measurably improves service predictability, not for theatre.

Marketplace ops + data-led expansion

Owned IT product, customer service, and the contractor supply side of an online marketplace during aggressive growth. National expansion (multi-city reach order-of-magnitude larger), mobile apps both sides, commercial booking product, fulfilment >99.85%, response times cut ~70%.

Under NDA with a data partner that had won grant-backed work: took churn-propensity signals, turned them into a national location heatmap, and overlaid where contractors actually lived. That map drove where to expand next — same pattern as any two-sided field marketplace. I am confident that twelve-month expansion window changed what the business was later worth to a buyer; runway was short, the acquisition happened, and I do not believe the same money would have been on the table without the footprint built in that year.

Subscription / DTC ops rebuild

Procurement-to-delivery rebuild in a growth sprint. JIT forecasting tied to manufacturing and freight lead times. Vertical capacity gain without a new building. WMS selection and location-driven barcode ops. National transport kept stable under scale.

ANZ ops + IT general management

Joint ownership of operations and IT — the join where most programmes fail. Continuity under commercial pressure, systems that match the floor.

Long-arc industrial & 3PL contracts

Ongoing innovation and optimisation across warehousing, logistics, transport, IT, and customer experience. Automation, relocation, procurement, KPI control, and commercial warehouse agreements — where drift hides when no one is looking.

AI — capability, ops fit, safeguards

I treat AI expertise as judgment under operational constraint, not as a content brand. Public work includes large-repository architecture reading (order of ~10k repos class of analysis: where systems break, where “AI breakpoints” live, when the architecture is the architecture), constitutional gates, and research that is honest about what is proven vs what is still open.

For clients, that becomes three services:

  1. Capability vs need — what this model/tool can actually do for this process, this labour model, this risk.
  2. Architecture & codebase interpretation — read what you already have; find where AI will fight the process or the governance.
  3. Safeguards — admission rules, identity-free signal packets where appropriate, human override, audit paths. Machines that act should not get a soft middle.
Most AI projects fail at the join: capability without operations, or operations without a hard stop. I work that join.

Good fit / bad fit

Good fit

  • Growth or chaos: the process and the systems are out of sync
  • Multi-site or multi-party handoffs failing
  • AI or automation proposed without an ops owner
  • Need someone who can talk floor, IT, and commercial in one room
  • Willing to measure and kill soft middles

Bad fit

  • Body-shop coding with no operational outcome
  • Strategy theatre with no executive owner

  • Unwilling to put a hard stop on automated action
